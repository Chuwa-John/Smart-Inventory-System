A day, from opening the shutter to locking up.

Nothing here asks you to change how you trade. It follows the way the day already goes.

Morning

Open the till

Whoever is on the counter writes down the money they are starting with. One person, one till.

Through the day

Sell, and the shelf follows

Every sale takes the item off the shelf count at the same moment. Nobody has to remember to do it twice.

When a regular asks

Give credit with a record

Put it on their name. Going past what you set for them needs a manager, and it is written down.

Evening

Count, and compare

Count the drawer. The difference is recorded whether it is short, over, or exactly right.

What it does.

Sales and stock together

Scan a barcode or search by name. The shelf count is a consequence of selling, not a separate chore.

Who owes you what

Customer balances, part payments, and an alert level you choose for each person.

More than one shop

Each branch keeps its own stock and its own till. Move goods between them and the move is recorded.

Staff who see their own work

A cashier sees the counter. A manager sees the branch. You see all of it.

Ask about your own numbers

What is not selling, what to reorder, what a slow week looked like — answered from your shop's figures.

Reports you can carry

Takings, staff, stock value. Save as PDF or spreadsheet and take it to the bank.

Most systems tell you what you sold.

The harder question is whether the money is actually there — and it is the one a notebook can never answer honestly, because a notebook can be rewritten after the fact.

One till, one person, one shift

Two people cannot both be responsible for the same drawer at the same time.

The sum is done for you

Opening money, plus cash sales, plus debts repaid in cash, less refunds. Then you count.

A short day stays short

Closing a shift cannot go back and change how it opened. That is the only reason the record is worth having.
